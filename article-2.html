A Field Guide to Common Unit Conversions

5 min read · Guides

Most unit conversions people look up fall into a handful of repeat categories. Knowing rough mental shortcuts for these means you often don't need a tool at all — and when you do, you'll spot an obviously wrong result faster.

Length: metric to imperial

The shortcut worth memorizing: 1 inch is close to 2.5 cm, and 1 mile is close to 1.6 km. For a fast mental estimate, multiply kilometers by 0.6 to get a rough mile figure — good enough for "is this walkable" decisions, not for anything requiring precision.

Weight: kilograms and pounds

1 kilogram is close to 2.2 pounds. A quick trick: double the kilogram figure and add 10% — 10 kg doubles to 20, plus 10% is 22, which matches the precise 22.05 lb closely enough for everyday use.

Temperature: the one that trips people up

Celsius to Fahrenheit isn't a simple multiply — it's (°C × 9/5) + 32. The fast mental version: double the Celsius figure and add 30. It's not exact, but it lands within a few degrees, which is usually enough to know whether to bring a jacket.

In practice: mental shortcuts are for quick decisions — packing, conversation, rough planning. For anything going into a document, recipe, or measurement that needs to be right, use the exact converter.

Why exact conversions still matter

Rounding errors compound. A shortcut that's off by 2% seems harmless on one measurement, but stacked across a recipe scaled up 10x, or a construction measurement repeated across a room, that small error becomes a real problem. Use shortcuts for judgment calls, and the calculator for anything you'll act on precisely.
